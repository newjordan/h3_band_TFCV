# Piano and drummer realism, 2026-10-10

This branch extends `8ec6cb3` in an isolated checkout. It does not replace the
whole-suite E36 result with a short-clip score. Generated evidence lives under
`artifacts/realism/`; final matched measurements are recorded below.

## What changed

- Finger solvers retain anatomical joint limits and now also enforce per-frame
  angular speed limits against the actual previous frame. The current engineering
  limit is 12 rad/s. A smoothed regularization pose is never mistaken for elapsed
  physical time during a repair pass.
- Different fingers collide through directional skin tubes derived from the
  existing cross-sections. Targets with high press weights no longer drown out
  separation. The model permits 0.5 mm of compression; proximal webbing is excluded.
- Contact gain rises before the strike, remains supported during a hold, and
  releases continuously. This is a dimensionless control gain, not simulated
  muscle force. Loudness changes the downstroke trajectory; pressing beneath the
  key bed is not a loudness mechanism.
- Finger targets, key actuation, and contact auditing use the same hinge lever.
  A key only moves when the pad lies on its finite exposed surface. White-key
  gaps and black-key occlusion count. Collision repair uses the actual solved key
  positions, so an assigned but missed note does not create an imaginary hole.
- A strong key-bed residual constrains all skin samples, including thumbs.
  Pressing-finger compression allowances only apply over the assigned exposed
  key. These are optimization residuals, not a proof of zero mesh penetration.
- Free-finger targets are transformed once per solve pass. Previously the second
  hand-collision iteration could transform them a second time.
- Wrist/distal forearm volumes participate in hand collision. Coupled arm IK
  preserves segment lengths for reachable targets, limits bend-plane changes,
  and uses offline lookahead to avoid late elbow switches. Infeasible targets and
  remaining collisions stay visible in `qa.arm_track`.
- Before the final finger solve, unreachable wrist references are projected into
  the actual two-bone workspace. The final audit compares arm lengths against the
  physical model across all exported frames, including warmup and tail; a
  consistently stretched limb cannot pass by matching its own median length.
- `blockout/drums.py` adds a drummer foundation: rigid sticks, fixed arm lengths,
  finite tilted drum surfaces, fingertip/grip proxies, strike/hold/rebound phases,
  exact event-time contact checks, and speed/collision failure gates. See
  [DRUMS.md](../blockout/DRUMS.md). This is not yet an anatomical skinned drummer.

## Reproduce

Use the project Python environment with NumPy/SciPy and matplotlib:

```sh
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python lab/realism_probe.py --source-root . --start 49.9 --dur 2 --warmup 1 --out artifacts/realism/debussy
python lab/realism_probe.py --source-root . --start 119.9 --dur 2 --warmup 1 --out artifacts/realism/wide_chord
python lab/realism_probe.py --source-root . --start 248 --dur 2 --warmup 1 --out artifacts/realism/overlap
python lab/realism_compare.py baseline.qa.json candidate.qa.json --out comparison.json
python -m blockout.drums --demo --out artifacts/realism/drums/demo.json
```

Probe defaults use the E36 recipe: scale 1, white press axes `[20,1,20]`, black
press axes `[20,20,20]`, two repairs, 3 mm target overshoot, and reach splitting.
The overshoot remains a target preload and is audited independently from actual
pad contact. Reports record source hashes, overrides, interval, warmup, and timing.
Frozen snapshots avoid comparing against source that changed during a run.

The old score only asked whether a key was at least half depressed near an onset.
The new audit also checks the pad's exposed-key footprint, signed surface gap,
held-note contact, release, joint limits/speeds, exported FK, bone lengths, finger
spacing, arm intersections, and reversals. The score-duration hold metric includes
intentional early releases in the motion plan; `controller_phase` separately
checks assigned firm phases. Capsule collision counts are conservative proxies.

## Original elbow glitch

The video starts at performed time 39.9 s. Its visible 248–252 s glitch therefore
corresponds to performed **287.9–291.9 s**, not performed 248–252 s. Fresh short
solves can choose different crossing roles, so replaying unchanged wrists from
the original `part_17.json` is required to assess that particular failure.

On the original 288–292 s, 96-frame replay, offline arm lookahead reduced maximum
elbow movement from 104.0 to 40.0 mm/frame and forearm-overlap frames from 63 to 24.
Arm lengths remained exact to numerical precision. Worst remaining overlap was
57.8 mm; maximum torso penetration was 0.35 mm inside the explicit 0.5 mm tolerance.
This is an improvement, not a collision-free result. Elbow changes cannot resolve
intersections pinned at wrist endpoints; those require wrist/hand planning changes.

## Measurement limits and remaining work

The hand solver uses sampled skin sections, not the full skinned mesh. Blender
mesh checks are necessary: coarse webbing triangles can fold even when distal
finger tubes clear, and triangle part attribution is approximate near blended
skin. Penetration depth must use the actual transformed key box and its nearest
face; a vertical heightfield overstates depth at key fronts and sides.

No hammer/action force model or tissue dynamics is simulated. No full-suite
revalidation or H3 render is implied by these focused tests. More passage context
can change fingering and crossing roles. Before promoting a final suite, run the
independent audit and mesh checks on that exact complete output, including stitches.
